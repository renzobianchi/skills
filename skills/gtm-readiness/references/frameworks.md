# Frameworks

## Product Fit Model

"PMF is when you can repeatably find, sell, serve, and retain customers with the same job-to-be-done." Four pillars, each tested for repeatability, each with a concrete signal:

| Pillar | Fit type | Question | PLG signal | Sales-led signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Find | Go-to-market | Can you consistently get in front of your ideal customers? | homepage traffic from the ICP | a first meeting |
| Sell | Go-to-market | Can you consistently close deals with your ICP? | signup | contract |
| Serve | Problem-solution | Can you consistently get customers to value? | activation | onboarding complete |
| Retain | Problem-solution | Do they stick around? | retention | renewal |

Retain course-corrects the others. Levels of fit: feature-market fit → product-market fit → platform-market fit; start with a single feature.

## Three phases of startup GTM

| Phase | Goal | How | Exit signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Experimentation | Learn who cares most about the problem | Sell by hand, speed and volume, many segments briefly | "A gut feeling that you could sell a lot of your product to a specific market" |
| Beachhead | Repeatability | One use case for one group; build the plumbing (homepage, decks, CRM, onboarding); ignore other markets | Repeatable and predictable motion, typically at least $1M ARR from the same segment |
| Expansion | Growth | Add adjacent markets with the same use case (horizontal) or new use cases for the same market (vertical); one segment at a time, each restarts beachhead | Each new segment reaches repeatability before the next |

Each segment costs a dedicated team: roughly $580k a year lean (product marketer, marketer, seller, customer success); seven segments run past $5M. Exception to sequencing: strong virality or community-led growth.

## Segment definition and prioritization

A marketable segment has five dimensions: company type, champion or department, job-to-be-done or use case, current tools or methods (the competitive alternative), and the problem in the champion's framing. Minimal viable market segment: workflow + alternative + problem; a customer with an alternative and no complaint is outside the ICP. Firmographics alone ("European EdTech SaaS with 1,000 employees") are not a segment.

Good segment: people evaluate products similarly, shop similarly, talk to each other, and are numerous enough for the goal.

Prioritize with four questions, size excluded: who is likely to stick around, who is easiest to reach, how easy are they to sell to, who is easiest to onboard. Segmentation-stage data: who was easiest to close, who gets the most joy, who shares the product.

## Market maturity

Immature (the activity is new → tie it to an unmet desired outcome), emerging (the activity exists, done with bad general tools → explain how your category is the best way), mature (many vendors → show core differentiation). A startup may span stages across segments.

## Stay broad or go specific

Five scenarios where broad works: instant aha, community-led growth, creator-led growth, famous founder, huge raise. Otherwise pick a prioritized segment. "If you have a distribution advantage, go broad. If you don't, go specific."

Two ways to cross the chasm: niche plus whole product (Gusto, Square), or borrowed credibility from millions of PLG users (Notion, Slack), which needs fast time to value, self-serve, virality, and millions of users.

## Category creation vs entering

If there is no comparison tool and no existing use case: position on a desired outcome, attack the problems of current tools, evangelize the use case, and only then the category. Categories form collectively (better tool → copycats → procurement and G2 need a bucket); skip the name-it-and-claim-it exercise. Category creation in practice is sub-category creation: cloud CRM, online bookstore, luxury EV.

## Growth model → where to focus

| Time to value | Implementation | ACV | Model | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| minutes | none | tiny | PLG | end user |
| 30 to 90 days | moderate | procurement | sales-led | middle management |
| 12+ months | long | 8 to 9 figures | consultant-driven | C-suite |

Website purpose by model: sales-led establishes credibility (logos, case studies); marketing-led converts one persona on a CTA with maximal clarity; PLG gets a trial with the most compelling capability and its feature. Early PLG behaves like marketing-led.

PLG messaging sequence: awareness (problem + use case) → acquisition (capabilities, at role level early) → activation (one workflow) → habit (benefits) → expansion (adjacent capabilities) → monetization.

## GTM types and platform paths

By use-case count: vertical, horizontal, vertical solutions, horizontal suite, platform; start vertical. Ways to sell a platform: one wedge plus upsell (least risky), multiple wedges per segment, bundle (riskiest). A platform only when buyers shop for several use cases at once. Expansion is horizontal (same use case, new industries) or vertical (same customers, adjacent use cases).

## Three strategies every startup needs

Business (where are you going), product (what do we build), positioning (how do we explain it). Positioning comes before product strategy.
