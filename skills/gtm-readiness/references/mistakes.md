# Mistakes catalogue

Walk top to bottom against the placement. Each entry: the mistake, why it fails, the correction, the example. List only those with evidence from the interview or the site.

1. **Claiming PMF from a few customers.** Not repeatable across find, sell, serve, retain. Test each pillar with numbers. "600+ startups, only partial progress."
2. **Skipping to the platform.** Nobody finds several fits at once. Find fit with one feature first. Stripe would have failed launching as "financial infrastructure for the internet."
3. **Running all three GTM phases at once.** Nothing reaches repeatability. Finish each phase before the next.
4. **Multi-segment GTM before repeatability.** Each segment needs its own team. One segment, then hire for the next. The horizontal death spiral: broad message, low conversion, more segments, thinner team.
5. **Firmographic-only ICP.** Not a marketable segment. Add workflow, alternative, problem.
6. **Picking the biggest segment.** Bad-fit customers pull the product apart and churn. Prioritize by sticks, reachable, sellable, onboardable.
7. **No segment, a too-broad one, a non-existent one, or several.** One size fits none. Choose the one you are winning in today.
8. **Solving a problem nobody owns.** No owner means nobody is looking and nobody has budget. Pick a problem someone owns, feels, and can act on. Asana started with team project management, not cross-department collaboration.
9. **Vision marketing to the mass market.** Early adopters buy the vision; the market buys the wedge. Hide the vision from customers. Bezos told investors "everything" and customers "books."
10. **Marketing the technology instead of the capability.** Customers care what it lets them do. Uber: "press a button and a car picks you up."
11. **Leading with outcomes or ROI.** People do not shop for outcomes; every vendor promises them. Lead with capabilities and first-order benefits. An outcome is differentiation only when guaranteed.
12. **Writing for the C-suite or the powerless end user.** Executives do not read homepages; end users cannot buy (PLG excepted). Write for the champion.
13. **Strategic narrative as the pitch.** "A big change in the world" is every deck. Distinguishable problem, better way, clear message.
14. **Branded terminology and invented categories.** Alienating; the buyer cannot shop it. Say what you do.
15. **Differentiation below the fold.** The thing nobody else has goes in the hero. Around buried it four scrolls down.
16. **Claiming better UX against the incumbent.** Buyers compare the whole product: support, integrations, compliance. Build the whole product for the niche.
17. **Copying big-brand marketing.** They have brand equity; your size is a liability. Sharper positioning, temporarily smaller product.
18. **Voice of the customer for messaging.** Existing customers use end-of-journey language; prospects do not. Use the voice of the prospect. Notion is bought as "a better notetaker."
19. **Homepage as company wiki.** Puts prospects into evaluation mode. The smallest message that gets the next step.
20. **Misreading market maturity.** A CRM saying "boost sales efficiency" in a mature market. Match the stage: outcome, use case, or category.
21. **Positioning only through a category lens.** Most of the market does not know the category exists. Map the JTBD alternatives: DIY, legacy tools, manual processes, agencies.
22. **Every value argument in one message.** Generic, bloated, forgettable; you get ten seconds. One primary alternative.
23. **Multi-channel, multi-tactic with a tiny team.** Nothing works. One ICP, one channel, one tactic.
24. **Content repurposing.** Mediocre assets everywhere. Repurpose the concept per channel.
25. **Interview podcast labeled demand gen.** It is brand marketing. Problem- or product-focused content sells.
26. **Marketing the roadmap.** Keep it away from GTM assets; it sells what does not exist.
27. **Interactive demo above the explanation.** Visitors need "what is it" first.
28. **Hating your hit.** Who gets remembered gets purchased; being known for one thing is a win. Arc and Dia; Slack's "AI work management."
29. **TAM, SAM, SOM as the market map.** Produces generic positioning and premature scaling. Use multi-dimensional segments.
30. **Founder delegating GTM.** Marketing teams are set up for failure without the founder's market knowledge. The founder owns GTM with sales and marketing leads.
