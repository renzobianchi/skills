# Rules

Run every candidate strategy and every one-sentence positioning through these. Each rule names the failure it prevents and an example from the source posts; cite the rule number in a candidate's risks when it applies.

1. **One thing about the market, one about the product.** Failure: "Connect everything. Achieve anything." (Airtable) says nothing. Correct: "a business bank account for startups" (Mercury).
2. **Lead with the clearest anchor, usually the capability.** Benefits and problems "could apply to countless products." Surfe leads with what it does.
3. **Never lead with a business outcome in a competitive category.** Every vendor claims it, and the founder now competes with "hire two more reps." "The CRM that drives business growth" (Pipedrive) is the failure.
4. **Diagnose market maturity before choosing the anchor.** Vanta in 2018: "Stop managing your SOC 2 audit in spreadsheets," not "the best SOC 2 automation platform," because the category did not exist yet.
5. **In an immature market the competitor is the status quo, not other startups.** Apideck positioned against building integrations in-house, not against Merge or Codat.
6. **One primary competitive alternative.** Combining every argument yields a message that is "generic, bloated, and forgettable." Airtable chose spreadsheets and databases.
7. **Name the enemy.** Without it prospects must "put two and two together." Freckle: "like Clay without the learning curve."
8. **Use an existing category when it explains you fastest.** "Intelligent Agreement Management" explains nothing; Miro is still "a digital whiteboard"; Snowflake reached a budget line as "the data warehouse built for the cloud."
9. **Category creation needs distribution (about a million impressions a month) and years of runway.** Apple can say "spatial computing"; a seed startup cannot.
10. **In a mature category, differentiate with a modifier or an unexpected feature.** "Smart" phone; folk CRM syncs LinkedIn contacts.
11. **In an immature category, lead with the problems of the current job and entry-level features, not advanced ones.** Ignition explained why you need a GTM platform before what theirs does.
12. **One category per page.** Zapier uses a different category per campaign; Jasper lists six on one page.
13. **Category does not set the price; segment and brand do.** A $12 Sony and a $5,000 Focal are both headphones.
14. **Go specific unless you have a distribution advantage.** Pick the highest-urgency use case.
15. **Position for the next revenue milestone, not the pitch deck.** Shopify: snowboard stores → sporting goods → SMB ecommerce.
16. **Own one use case before many.** Gusto owned payroll before benefits and HR.
17. **"Enterprise" is not a segment.** Whole-product needs differ by industry; pick one.
18. **One persona, one narrative.** Two narratives on one page produce "a watered-down message that appeals to no one." Supabase speaks to technical users, not to vibe coders.
19. **The problem must have an owner with budget.** "Everyone's problem, and therefore no one's." Asana started with team project management, not cross-department collaboration.
20. **Show the struggling moment, not the meta-problem.** "Data is disconnected" cannot be pictured; feedback in emails cross-referenced against Sketch can.
21. **Every claim survives "our buyers won't believe that."** Apideck dropped "every accounting integration pre-built" for the work developers actually skip: OAuth, token handling, data mapping.
22. **Broader positioning adds competitors; it removes none.** A scheduling tool that adds email marketing now fights Mailchimp too.
23. **Position what you sell, not the technology you use.** "We're an AI company" selling shoes. AI is table stakes.
24. **Positioning goes on the homepage; it is not A/B tested.** Sales-led validation takes about two years and a test signals no conviction. Calendly held one line for eleven years.
25. **Clarity before differentiation.** "You only get to differentiation after you have solved for clarity"; clarity alone won Fletch its first hundred customers.
26. **A strategy the team would not put in the hero will not be brought to life.** The hero test is the commitment test.
27. **Do not claim the outcome unless you guarantee it.** An outcome claim differentiates only when it is a guarantee; otherwise it is a promise every competitor also makes.
