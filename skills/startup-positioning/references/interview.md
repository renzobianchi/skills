# Interview question bank

Ask two or three at a time. Each question carries the follow-up that catches the vague answer, because the first answer is usually the pitch deck. Wording is Fletch's where it exists.

## Readiness gate

- "Do you have any customers?" → "Are these customers in a variety of segments, or mostly one?"
- "Are you able to identify a best-fit customer segment and persona?" → if yes, name it now; it is the round-2 hypothesis.
- "Are you willing to commit to having your homepage messaging speak ONLY to them for the next 6 months?" → a hedge ("we can't exclude anyone") is a no.
- "Are you willing to devote 80% of your go-to-market efforts to reaching that single segment for the next 6 months?"
- "Why are we doing this?" (expanding into a new segment, pivoting, tuning what works)
- "Where are we today?" (paying customers, ARR, churn) → "What go-to-market resources do we have?" (reps, marketers, capital) → "What is the revenue goal to hit in the next 6 to 18 months?"

## Round 1, core four

- "What is your product?" → if the answer is a vision or an outcome: "What category would a buyer put you in, or what is the leading use case?"
- "Who is it for?" → if a demographic: "Who is the person whose job it is to fix this, and what kind of company are they in?"
- "What does it replace?" → if "nothing, we're new": "What were your last three customers doing before they bought?"
- "Why are you better?" → if a list of adjectives: "Better than what, specifically, and what feature makes that true?"
- "Where are you winning the most today?" (segment, use case, channel)

## Round 2, the customer

- "What do people do with your product?" (activity; never "why", which yields outcomes, nor "how", which yields features) → test: "Could you build an onboarding flow for that?"
- "How does the customer currently carry out this use case?" → "Are they using a rival product, or carrying out some process?"
- "What are the limitations of doing it this way?" → should contrast with a capability; if it does not, the differentiation is not there yet.
- "What struggles do they encounter in this context?" → push to the struggling moment: "Tell me about the last time that happened to a customer: what day, what meeting, what did they open?"
- Ownership: "Does anyone actually own solving X? Is it anyone's job? Is anyone looking? Does anyone have budget for solving it?"
- Demo trigger: "But that's not the reason people book demos with you, right?" → the real trigger is the problem to position on.
- "What's the outcome of doing it your way?" → keep it first-order: "What changes the same week they start using it?"

## Round 3, the market

- Maturity: "If I asked ten of your target customers how they solve this today, would most name the same product category, would they name spreadsheets and email, or would they say they don't do it yet?"
- "Does your startup harness existing energy, or are you trying to get people to leave the starting line?"
- Category: "How do you refer to these companies, the ones prospects compare you with?" → "If you find yourself saying 'we're so much more than a ___', that blank is your category."
- "Would they know exactly what category you are in, or what main workflow you support?"
- JTBD map: "List every way someone gets this job done without you: DIY, a legacy tool bent to it, a manual process, an agency, a vendor in your category." → "Which one are you PRIMARILY positioning against, and why that one?"
- Distribution: "Do you have a distribution advantage (community, audience, virality, a big raise)?" → no advantage means go specific.

## Round 4, the segment

For each candidate segment:
- "Is there a compelling reason to buy?" (problem severity)
- "Are we significantly better than the alternative(s) they use?" (differentiation)
- "Can you easily and consistently get in front of them?" (access)
- "Does this segment support your short-term revenue goal and burn?" (size, last)
- "Who sticks around? Who was easiest to close? Who gets the most joy from the product? Who shares it?"
- Anti-persona: "Who is your product NOT for? Who buys it and churns?"

## Before the pick

- "Why SHOULD you make this bet?" "Why SHOULDN'T you?"
- "Would you put this hero on the homepage tomorrow?" → hesitation means the strategy will not be brought to life.
- "Would your buyers believe that line?" → drop any claim that fails.
